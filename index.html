About

I am a PhD student in Finance at McMaster University. My research examines how financial markets, credit institutions, and changes in asset values shape the financial decisions and economic outcomes of households and firms.

I am particularly interested in household finance, consumer lending, and financial intermediation. My work studies how collateral values, borrower financial positions, and lending arrangements influence credit outcomes, repayment decisions, and default.

I also investigate broader questions in corporate finance and asset pricing, including how technological change and firms' contractual structures affect the distribution and maturity of financial risk.

Research Interests

Household Finance and Consumer Credit: Household borrowing and repayment decisions, consumer credit markets, collateral equity, loan prepayment, default, and the interaction between household balance sheets and asset price fluctuations.

Financial Intermediation and Credit Markets: Lending institutions, credit origination, borrower selection, underwriting practices, loan servicing, and the allocation of credit across households.

Corporate Finance and Asset Pricing: Technological change and financial risk, artificial intelligence exposure, contractual rigidity, cash-flow maturity, and the term structure of expected returns.

Empirical Finance: Applied econometrics, large-scale financial datasets, institutional settings, and the use of granular data to investigate economic mechanisms in credit and financial markets.

Research in Progress

Collateral Equity and Borrower Decisions in Auto Credit Markets
Household Finance | Consumer Credit

This project examines how borrowers' equity positions in financed vehicles relate to loan repayment and exit decisions. Using auto loan asset-backed securities data, I investigate the relationship between collateral values, loan-to-value positions, prepayment, and default. The analysis explores how changes in vehicle prices are associated with borrower credit outcomes.

Mortgage Lock-In and the Timing of Childbearing
Household Finance | Housing Finance

This project investigates whether mortgage-related constraints affect household fertility decisions. It examines how mortgage exposure and housing market conditions interact with household mobility and the timing of childbearing, highlighting the potential demographic consequences of financial frictions in housing markets.

Artificial Intelligence Exposure and the Term Structure of Expected Returns
Asset Pricing | Corporate Finance

This project examines how firms' exposure to artificial intelligence may affect the maturity structure of expected stock returns. It explores the interaction between technological uncertainty, contractual rigidity, operating leverage, and the timing of expected cash flows.
